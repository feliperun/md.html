# The bell that did not ring

The fog came in before the tide did, rolling over the harbor in low gray waves.

Elena had kept the light at Calm Point for nine winters. She knew every stair, every draft, and every groan of the lamp mechanism — which was why the silence in the tower felt wrong.

::: quote | the keeper's ledger
A lighthouse does not chase the ships. It simply stays lit.
:::

## The parcel

The parcel arrived on a Tuesday, wrapped in oilcloth and tied with twine. It was addressed to the previous keeper, a man the ledger called only *S*. He had left no address, no forwarding note, and no boat.

"Who sends a letter to a ghost?" Elena asked the empty room.

The wind answered with a low moan against the glass.

## The name

Inside the parcel were three books and a letter. The letter was short, the handwriting small and precise, and it ended with a name she had not heard in a decade: her own.[^ledger]

[^ledger]: The parish register records only initials; the hand is the previous keeper's, and the ink matches the logbook.

She closed the parcel. The lamp above her hummed, patient, indifferent.

## The climb

At the top of the tower, the brass bell hung silent. It had rung once — on the night the old keeper vanished — and never since.

Elena touched the clapper.

It was warm.

Then, below her, the door of the lighthouse swung shut, and a key turned in the lock.

*Someone else was on the island.*
